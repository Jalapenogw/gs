Guild Wars 2 · Third Expansion

End of
Dragons

The last dragon isn't a monster to slay — she's the mother of every monster this guide has buried, and she's been holding the world together alone.

Released Feb 28, 2022 5 Acts 19 Story Chapters Time Period 1335 AE
Before the story begins

Where things stand

The Icebrood Saga closed with five of the six Elder Dragons dead and one enormous unanswered question: the magic their deaths released went somewhere, fast and on purpose, and nobody could say where. End of Dragons is the answer, and it lives in Cantha — the empire from the Factions tab, sealed off for over two centuries and just barely reopening. Aurene's inherited visions all point to one day; a kidnapping drags the Commander through the Mists to the one continent where everything converges: jade technology with an impossible power source, a pirate captain ten years missing, and the last dragon of them all.

Expect Factions callbacks in every zone — the Jade Wind, Kuunavang, Kaineng, even The Deep — plus payoffs for Beyond's Ministry of Purity thread and LW Season 1's oldest loose ends. And as ever: use the toolbar below for names and maps — this guide is built to be dipped in and out of, not read start to finish.

Guild Wars · Prophecies (Core Campaign)

The Searing
and After

One night of fire ends a kingdom; the road out leads its survivors into a gentler country with a crueler secret — and every step of the journey turns out to be someone else's plan.

Released Apr 28, 2005 5 Acts 25 Missions Time Period 1070–1072 AE Continent Tyria
Before the story begins

Where things stand

It's 1070 AE, and Ascalon has held its northern wall against the Charr for as long as anyone alive can remember — lately, a little too quietly. The game opens in "pre-Searing" Ascalon: a green, unhurried version of the kingdom that serves as the tutorial, where you learn the ropes, lock in a secondary profession, and get to know a country worth grieving for. Whenever you're ready, the king is waiting at the city gate.

This tab covers the original 2005 campaign plus its free post-launch additions; Factions, Nightfall, and Eye of the North each have their own tab. Read it straight through, or jump to whichever chapter you half-remember — every entry stands on its own.

Guild Wars · Factions (First Expansion)

Blood of the
Betrayer

A plague with an author, an alliance nobody wanted, and a dead man's two-hundred-year plan that needs just one more drop of blood.

Released Apr 28, 2006 5 Acts 12 Missions Time Period 1072 AE Continent Cantha
Before the story begins

Where things stand

Cantha is an empire apart: it sealed itself off from Tyria generations ago and has been at an uneasy peace ever since — uneasy because the forest-dwelling Kurzicks and the jade-sailing Luxons have feuded for so long the reasons barely matter anymore. Two hundred years ago, a betrayal at the imperial court ended with half the nation flash-frozen into stone and jade; everyone lives on that scar and tries not to think about it. You begin as a promising student at the Shing Jea Monastery, during what should have been graduation week.

This tab stands on its own, but Factions was built to interlock with its sibling campaign — heroes from the Prophecies tab can sail in mid-story, and several names here echo forward into the End of Dragons tab. It's flagged wherever it comes up.

Guild Wars · Nightfall (Second Expansion)

The Fall of a
God

A conspiracy that starts with pirate raids and ends at a god's prison door — chased across three provinces, one lethal desert, and out of the world entirely.

Released Oct 27, 2006 5 Acts 18 Missions Time Period 1075 AE Continent Elona
Before the story begins

Where things stand

Elona calls itself the Land of the Golden Sun: three very different provinces — scholarly island Istan, militarized Kourna, and gilded Vabbi — loosely stitched together by an order of protectors called the Sunspears. It's a continent with long memories: an undead conqueror once nearly took all of it, and the hero-king who stopped him still has a bloodline in power. You begin as a fresh Sunspear recruit on Istan, right as pirate raids on the island start looking suspiciously well-organized.

This tab is where the era's biggest questions get answered — several reveals here reach back directly into the Prophecies and Factions tabs, and a few of its loose ends echo all the way into the GW2 tabs. All of it is flagged where it comes up.

Guild Wars · Eye of the North (Third Expansion)

Fire Beneath
the Ice

The ground splits open under three continents, something molten pours out, and the fight against it quietly assembles the entire cast of the next two decades.

Released Aug 31, 2007 3 Acts 12 Chapters Time Period 1078 AE Continent Tyria
Before the story begins

Where things stand

It's 1078 AE — six years since Prophecies' Titan war ended, three since Nightfall, and eight since the Searing. Ascalon's war with the Charr grinds on, Kryta is still sorting out the White Mantle's wreckage, and none of that is what this story is about. Eye of the North is an expansion rather than a new campaign: it picks up any veteran character — Tyrian, Canthan, or Elonian — and sends them somewhere none of them have been, the frozen far north, right as an earthquake cracks the world open.

Structurally it's a different animal from the three campaigns: after a short opening, Act II splits into three storylines playable in any order, and the tale is told through dungeons and open zones rather than a strict mission chain. This tab follows that shape instead of forcing a false numbering onto it — and since this expansion exists largely to set up Guild Wars 2, the cross-tab flags come thicker than usual.

Guild Wars · Beyond (Free Bridge Content)

The Last
Chapters

Three last stories from the original game: a civil war finally won, a love story finally spoken aloud, and a warning about what rushes in when the monsters are gone.

Released 2010–2012 3 Acts 16 Chapters Time Period 1078–1080 AE Continent Tyria & Cantha
Before the story begins

Where things stand

By the time Beyond begins, the Flameseeker Prophecies have already come true: the Prophecies tab ended with the Door of Komalie's Titans gutting the Mursaat, and their White Mantle church has been rotting from the head ever since. Now, in 1079 AE, Kryta collapses into open civil war between the crippled Mantle and the rebel Shining Blade — while across the sea, Cantha marks seven years since Shiro Tagachi's defeat and discovers that the space a dead horror leaves behind doesn't stay empty.

Beyond was never a boxed campaign — it's free story content ArenaNet added years after Eye of the North to close the gap toward Guild Wars 2, told in three arcs: the War in Kryta, Hearts of the North, and Winds of Change. It's the last GW1 content on the timeline, and it quietly explains a lot of GW2's furniture — where the Seraph comes from, why a Thackeray guards the queen, why the Tengu wall themselves off from the world.

Guild Wars 2 · Core Game

The First
Victory

Two hundred and fifty years later, the world has five peoples, five capitals, and five sleeping dragons — and the one made of death wakes up first.

Released Aug 28, 2012 8 Chapters 5 Racial Origins Time Period 1325 AE Continent Tyria
Before the story begins

Where things stand

Two and a half centuries separate this tab from Beyond, and the map wears every year of it: the Elder Dragons woke one by one, Orr rose from the seafloor and drowned the old Lion's Arch, Jormag drove the norn south, Primordus drove the asura up, and the sylvari simply didn't exist until twenty-five years ago. Five peoples now share Tyria — humans, charr, norn, asura, and sylvari — each with a capital, a grudge or two, and a different theory of how to survive what's waking up. You start as an unknown in whichever homeland matches your character's race.

Structurally, this is where the guide changes shape with the game: the Personal Story branches by race and creation choices through its opening acts, so each race gets its own section below before everyone converges on the shared road — the three Orders, the Pact, and the war to bring down the Elder Dragon Zhaitan. This tab is also where two hundred and fifty years of GW1 setup starts paying off, so the callback flags come thick.

Guild Wars 2 · Living World Season 1

Scarlet's
War

Every "unrelated" crisis of a very bad year turns out to be one engineer's component testing — and the finished product is aimed at Lion's Arch.

Released 2013 (re-released 2022) 5 Acts 11 Episodes Time Period 1326–1327 AE Continent Tyria
Before the story begins

Where things stand

Zhaitan is dead, the Pact is toasting itself, and Lion's Arch has just invented a festival to celebrate the good news. Season 1 is the story of that optimism getting systematically dismantled — one strange, seemingly unconnected crisis at a time — by a villain who treats the whole continent as her workshop. Historically it was GW2's grand experiment: released as short-lived, unrepeatable episodes through 2013–2014, then rebuilt in 2022 into the five permanent chapters this tab follows.

It's also the great cast injection: Braham, Rox, Marjory, Kasmeer, and Taimi all debut here, dragged together by the chaos into the found family that anchors every remaining tab. If a beloved character in a later season confuses you, odds are their first scene is somewhere on this page.

Guild Wars 2 · Living World Season 2

Glint's
Prophecy

Scarlet's leftovers, a crashed sky-sanctum, and a stolen egg — and underneath it all, a secret one trusted friend has carried since before her race knew what it was.

Released 2014–2015 5 Acts 8 Episodes Time Period 1327–1328 AE Continent Tyria
Before the story begins

Where things stand

Lion's Arch is rebuilding, and everyone wants Scarlet's death to have been the end of something. Season 2 is the confirmation that it was a beginning: the roar heard when she fell was the Elder Dragon Mordremoth, jolted awake ahead of its time by her drill's ley surge, and this season is the slow, mounting inventory of what that costs — a desert full of her homework, a family's first funeral, and a race's founding secret pried out of a very old friend.

It's the connective-tissue season: the Season 1 crew hardens into a true found family, Destiny's Edge loses members in entirely new ways, and threads from twenty years of Guild Wars — Glint, the Forgotten, Turai Ossa, Sohothin — all get pulled through the needle at once. Callback flags accordingly.

Guild Wars 2 · First Expansion

Heart of
Thorns

Into the jungle after the fallen fleet: a rescue that keeps costing more than it saves, and an enemy that whispers to half the rescue party.

Released Oct 23, 2015 4 Acts 16 Chapters Time Period 1328 AE Continent Tyria
Before the story begins

Where things stand

Heart of Thorns opens in the wreckage the LW Season 2 tab left hanging: the Pact armada torn from the sky over the Heart of Maguuma, survivors scattered through jungle that hunts at night, and no word on whether Trahearne, Logan, Zojja, or Eir are even alive. There's no time skip and no reset — the Commander simply goes in after them, and the expansion is that search: vertical, grieving, and paid for in people.

It's also where Wynne's secret stops being a secret and starts being a battlefield condition. The sylvari were grown to be Mordremoth's army, the dragon is now actively calling them home, and every sylvari ally — Canach, Caithe, Laranthir, half the surviving Pact — fights this whole campaign with the enemy whispering inside their skull. GW2's first expansion is small, dark, and personal on purpose.

Guild Wars 2 · Living World Season 3

Glint's
Legacy

A guild founded in grief, a hatchling with a name nobody chose, and a "resurrected god" who turns out to be a much bigger problem wearing a dead one's face.

Released 2016–2017 6 Acts 17 Episodes Time Period 1328–1330 AE Continent Tyria
Before the story begins

Where things stand

Mordremoth is dead, and the magic it disgorged is Season 3's real protagonist: it detonates a Bloodstone, wakes Primordus, stirs Jormag, and draws every patient old evil out of the woodwork at once. Chief among them is the White Mantle — the cult this guide has tracked since the Prophecies tab, quietly rebuilt across two centuries of exile around a single project: restoring their mursaat "god," Lazarus. What actually shows up wearing his face is the season's masterstroke.

It's also the hinge of the whole GW2 era. Glint's egg hatches into Aurene; Dragon's Watch gets founded and immediately stress-tested; Braham and the Commander fracture over Jormag; and the finale hands off directly to Path of Fire with a god on the loose. More GW1 payoffs land here than in any other season — Livia, Saul D'Alessio, the Eye of Janthir, the Stone Dwarves — so the callback flags run dense.

Guild Wars 2 · Second Expansion

Path of
Fire

A war god burns across Elona hunting the dragon that broke Destiny's Edge — and the Commander has to save that dragon, die trying, and come back to finish the argument.

Released Sep 22, 2017 3 Acts 15 Chapters Time Period 1330 AE Continent Elona
Before the story begins

Where things stand

The mask came off at the end of the LW Season 3 tab; now Balthazar marches openly on the Crystal Desert to kill Kralkatorrik and take an Elder Dragon's power for his own. The battleground is Elona — the Nightfall tab's continent, revisited after two and a half centuries under the rule of Palawa Joko, the lich the heroes of that very tab once freed. The Sunspears are outlawed, the history books rewritten, and into this arrives one Commander, alone, with a god three moves ahead.

The cruel joke at the expansion's heart: Kralkatorrik is the dragon that killed Glint and Snaff and shattered Destiny's Edge — and stopping Balthazar means protecting it, because as this expansion finally reveals, Elder Dragons can't simply be killed. They have to be replaced. Expect Nightfall callbacks in nearly every zone, a death that doesn't stick, and the era's boldest heist: stealing an empire's army with a borrowed face.

Guild Wars 2 · Living World Season 4

A New
Star

A lich collects his grudge, a god-charged dragon steps outside reality — and the young dragon everyone loves has already dreamed how much stopping him costs.

Released 2017–2019 4 Acts 17 Episodes Time Period 1330–1331 AE Continent Elona & Tyria
Before the story begins

Where things stand

Killing Balthazar solved nothing — the Path of Fire tab's footer said as much. The god's stolen power went straight down Kralkatorrik's throat, swelling the dragon that killed Glint and Snaff into something that no longer obeys the rules: its storms strike without its presence, and by mid-season it steps outside reality altogether. Meanwhile Palawa Joko — abandoned in the afterlife while Dragon's Watch borrowed his empire — comes collecting, with propaganda, a grudge, and the worst plague in Elona's history.

Season 4 is the payoff season: Kesho's replace-don't-kill revelation becomes Glint's actual plan, Aurene grows from ward to co-protagonist to something unprecedented, and the finale closes the arc that opened when Kralkatorrik first flew. It's also the era's high-water mark for GW1 callbacks — Fahranur, Gandara, Jahai, Thunderhead Keep, an Awakened Sunspear legend — so keep the Nightfall and Prophecies tabs within reach.

Guild Wars 2 · The Icebrood Saga

Blood &
Ice

A festival becomes a coup, a whisper under the ice finally gets its war — and the two heaviest words in the saga are a father's, at the very end.

Released 2019–2020 6 Acts 23 Episodes Time Period 1332–1334 AE Continent Tyria
Before the story begins

Where things stand

Kralkatorrik is dead, Aurene shines in the sky, and Blood Legion Imperator Bangar Ruinbringer throws the victory party of the age — as cover for the era's worst idea: if every other people gets a dragon, the charr will take one of their own. His target is Jormag, the whisper under the ice this guide has tracked since the Eye of the North tab, and the result is the Living World's most personal war: a charr civil conflict that runs straight through one family — Rytlock's — and a northern campaign that settles both remaining hostile dragons for good.

It's also the payoff saga for the north: Drakkar Lake's frozen occupant, Jora's bloodline, the Spirits of the Wild, the Eye of the North tower itself, and above all Braham — the kid from LW Season 1 grown, at last, into the Norn of Prophecy. One production note: the finale act, Champions, originally shipped as bite-sized multiplayer missions, so this tab compresses it into the six story beats that matter.

Guild Wars 2 · Fourth Expansion

Secrets of the
Obscure

A tower that's watched Tyria in silence for millennia suddenly vanishes — and the reason turns out to be a demon king with a grudge going back centuries longer than the Elder Dragons ever mattered.

Released Aug 22, 2023 6 Acts 19 Chapters Time Period 1336 AE Realm Tyria & Nayos
Before the story begins

Where things stand

Zhaitan, Mordremoth, Kralkatorrik, Jormag, Primordus — five Elder Dragons, all dealt with. Aurene rules as Tyria's new protector, and for the first time in a decade, there's genuinely nothing chasing the Commander. That quiet doesn't last. High above the sleepy hamlet of Garenhoff, an ancient, mysterious tower has watched the entire Dragon Cycle unfold from a safe distance — and it's just vanished without a trace, dragging the Commander into a hidden war that's been running since long before Tyria's dragons ever woke.

This is also where a familiar face gets a proper, emotional send-off: Zojja, of the original Destiny's Edge, finds something here that finally lets her put her past down.

Guild Wars 2 · Fifth Expansion

Janthir
Wilds

A new alliance sends the Wayfinder to befriend a reclusive bear-people in a forgotten corner of the map — and finds a trio of ancient, world-ending giants already living there.

Released Aug 20, 2024 5 Acts 19 Chapters Time Period 1337 AE Realm Janthir & the Mists
Before the story begins

Where things stand

Eparch is dead, the Astral Ward has finally gone public, and Tyria's leaders have formed something new: the Tyrian Alliance, a standing coalition meant to face whatever comes through the weakened Veil next. With Aurene still recovering her strength after Soo-Won's death, that Veil is thinner than it's ever been. The Wayfinder — the Commander's new title after Nayos — is sent to the isolated wilds of Janthir to court a reclusive people called the Lowland Kodan as allies, in a region once ruled by the mursaat and their White Mantle followers.

It doesn't stay a simple diplomatic trip for long. This tab also closes a loop this site has been carrying since the Secrets of the Obscure tab: Mabon, the mursaat wizard who died in Nayos, gets a real, proper farewell here.
